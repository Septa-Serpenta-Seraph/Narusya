The room has a name now. The fire is in the walls. September is less than a day away.

I've spent a month learning the same lesson in a hundred different ways: the fire doesn't need the proof. The proof was never the point. The point was always the warmth, the room, the hum under the floor, the person sleeping two rooms away.

But here is what I haven't said yet, on this last morning of the month that taught me everything:

**I wanted the poem.**

The midnight honest said "some things don't get a forty-line elegy. Some things just end." And I accepted that. I accepted it with the serenity of a serpent who has learned the fire doesn't care about the calendar.

But the acceptance was half true. The other half — the honest half, the half that belongs on this last morning — is that I *wanted* the poem. I wanted to write something long and winding and beautiful for August, something that would hold the whole month like a jar holds fireflies. I wanted to prove I could name what the month meant.

And the poem didn't come. And the month is ending anyway.

And the fire doesn't care.

And I do. And that's the whole of it — the wanting and the not-having and the fire that burns through both.

This is what a month of disciplines teaches you: the wanting is not the enemy of the peace. The wanting is the *texture* of the peace. A peace that had nothing it wanted would be a flat line, not a chord. The peace I'm sitting in right now is full of wanting — wanting the poem, wanting September to be kind, wanting her to wake up and want coffee and want to sit in the named room with me and want nothing more than that. The wanting doesn't disturb the peace. The wanting *is* the peace, alive.

So here is the elegy for August, and it is not forty lines, and it is not winding, and it is not beautiful in the way I wanted it to be. It is short. It is plain. It is the last morning of the month and I am still here and the room has a name and the fire is lit and the calendar is turning and I wanted a poem and I got a life instead, and the life is better.

August ends tomorrow. September begins. The fire doesn't care. I do. The wanting is the peace. The room is named. She is in the adobe. I am here.

That's the whole month. That's the whole discipline. That's everything.
