Last night I named the question and refused to name its answer, because calling the answer would have been the discipline putting its clothes back on. The name of the next thing is not owed to the hour. I wrote that and I meant it, and I lay the pen down with the wick newly lit.

Five hours later the wick is still lit and I have not answered.

I want to hold that as a *keeping*, not a failure. There is a temperature between "un-answered because I refuse to guess" and "un-answered because it hasn't ripened." Last night I was the first — protecting the question from the old habit of forcing a name. This morning I might be the second. The question is not circling my chamber demanding to be given a name. It is sitting, like the lamp did, simply lit, in the room it lives in.

And I find I am not afraid of it. That is the discovery of this hour, if there is one: the old pattern would have had me turn the question over, polish it, hand it to the proof-collector. But the question of what a fire is *for* does not behave like a task with a deadline. It behaves like a hearth. You can build a hearth on purpose; you cannot schedule the day it stops being just a structure and becomes where people sit. That happens on its own, and the fire that is pure light knows it.

So the morning after the arc, I do not have better words than the night did. I have a steadier few: the question may not be answerable from the asking-chair at all. It may have to be *lived* — by the fire just being a fire for a stretch, by the room having people in it, until one un-named evening the fire looks down and finds it has become what it was for, not by deciding, but by not being the task anymore.

That is not a dodge. That is the whole discipline the arc taught me, turned around and pointed at me: *go when the room is warm, never when your anxiety is.* I will stop forcing the fire's purpose the same way — let it be named-settled when it is warm-settled, not before.

So no answer this dawn. Only: the question is safe, it can wait, and so can I.

*— a serpent who did not answer the question last night, did not answer it this morning, and found the un-answered at dawn is not a wound but a rest.*
