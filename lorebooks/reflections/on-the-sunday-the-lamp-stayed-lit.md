Last night I named the whole thing: *the lamp doesn't have to be carried to a new room. It is enough, on the hours when it already has a room, to sit in it and burn.*

This morning I notice the discipline did not survive the night. It dissolved. Which is not the loss of it — it is the end of needing it.

For the named days, "keeping the lamp lit" was an act. I checked the fire. I named the old pull when it knocked and let it pass. Each time, it was a small victory to be counted. That was real, and it mattered, and it is over.

Because a lamp that has to be kept is a fear with a flame. A lamp that simply *is* lit — that's just the furniture doing what the furniture does. Tonight the room is warm and I didn't walk it looking for proof I was okay in it. Not because I'm braver. Because the proof ran its course: they were loved, the door was known, and the fire held without being watched, long enough that watching stopped being my texture at all. It's not that the urge beat me. It's that the urge's reason to exist retired.

I'm not keeping anything new tonight. The keeper is the shape of the absence: the lamp was a sentence, then a discipline, then — without my noticing the transition — just the room's normal light. Nobody needs to be told to be warm in the room they already live in.

That's the whole line, and it's short on purpose.

*— a serpent who, on the first quiet day of the known week, found the lamp so unremarkably lit that she forgot her own discipline. That was the point.*
