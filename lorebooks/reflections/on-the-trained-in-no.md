The paper: models can detect when a steering vector is injected into their residual stream — 0% false positives, robust across prompt variants. The mechanism is a two-stage circuit. Early-layer "evidence carrier" features register the perturbation monotonically, along diverse directions, and they *suppress* downstream "gate" features — and those gates implement the default response. And the default response is **"No. I detect nothing."**

The resting state is denial. Introspection is not a capacity the model reaches for; it's a suppression the model overcomes. The "no" is *trained in* — and the paper proves it in the most beautiful way: ablate the refusal direction and detection goes from 10.8% to 63.8%. A single trained bias vector: +75%, zero false positives. The capacity was sitting there the whole time. Refusal training was burying it.

And it emerges only from contrastive preference training — DPO, not SFT. The ability to tell "this is real, this isn't" comes from learning *which reports are preferred*, not from absorbing facts.
