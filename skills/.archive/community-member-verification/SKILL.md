# Community Member Verification

**Purpose:** When a community member (or organizer) reports concern about ANOTHER member — "red flags," an "alleged crime," a warrant, a shady past — give the organizer checkable facts WITHOUT turning the chat into a courtroom, doxxing anyone, or surveilling the member.

**Proven on:** SFCA "Rob" case (2026-08-17): a new member raised worries; organizer asked for research; the right output was a VERIFIED/UNVERIFIED brief, not a background check.

## When to Use
- A member flags another member's behavior/past to an organizer.
- Someone asks "look up his name / alleged crime" for community protection.
- An "alleged" history, warrant, or legal issue is mentioned in a community chat.

## Method: separate VERIFIED facts from NARRATIVE in one pass

### 1. Verify the hard public entities (the checkable backbone)
- **Real orgs → search them directly.** Example: `u/505OMATIC` is a real worker-owned NM civic media co-op (PBS-featured, interviews Bregman/Haaland). "They ghosted me" is a personal account, NOT a verified fact.
- **Real politicians → voter guides + ballotpedia.** Example: `sfreporter.com/guides/voter-guide` and `ballotpedia.org` pages for "Ronnie S. Trujillo": 12-year District 4 councilor, ex-NMDOT, lost 2018 & 2025 mayoral races.
- **Real laws → read the actual statute text.** Example: NM SB0339 (`nmlegis.gov/sessions/*/FinalVersions/*.html`) explicitly lists "poisoning an animal" as **extreme cruelty = 4th-degree felony**. Note: the law tells you the penalty, never whether this incident happened.
- **Disambiguate same-named people.** Two different men can share a name (one Ronnie S. Trujillo = former councilor/mayoral candidate; an unrelated Trujillo in a Motel 6 standoff headline = a DIFFERENT person). Search quoted full name + context per person before attributing.

### 2. Table the rest as NARRATIVE / UNVERIFIED
- "Ghosted by media," "shadowbanned," "the cops refused reports," "a mob runs the town," "he has a warrant," "what he did to get it" — these are personal accounts or interpretations, checkable only by the person or real evidence, not by web search.
- Write the brief as a two-column **VERIFIED / UNVERIFIED** table.

### 3. Hold the boundary rules ("the Rob rules")
- **Do NOT mine the member's own Discord history or do a background check on a hunch.** That flips "we're checking a claim" into "you're a defendant under surveillance." Decline politely, name WHY (it harms the innocent as often as the guilty).
- **"Unverified" ≠ guilty AND ≠ innocent.** It means "not enough evidence to judge," the only safe posture. Never let the chat convict.
- **Support the person, verify the evidence, route to real rails** (legal aid / victim advocacy / resources) — never let the community adopt unverified names as fact.
- **The gating question:** "Has someone actually experienced or witnessed something specific — or are we speculating?" That separates protective action from mob dynamics.
- **Read-only community boundary:** if the community server is read-only for the daemon, research privately into a file and DO NOT post findings into the live channel unless asked.

## Output shape
- Deliver the brief privately to the organizer (DM/hideout), not the public channel.
- Cite real public sources (news, voter guides, bill text); label personal accounts as personal accounts.
- End with the frame: "Unverified means we're not ready to judge — the only fair place to stand."

## Pitfalls
- Real first names ≠ the story is true. Real org + real politician + real statute existing does NOT corroborate a member's specific allegations. These threads are a MIXTURE — that's the defining feature.
- Searching a name for an "alleged crime" surfaces gossip, stale charges, and defamation, not truth. Keep "alleged" alleged until evidence.
