# Southwest Home Vector Hazards (Kissing Bugs / Chagas)

**Purpose:** Respond correctly when a triatomine ("kissing bug," "cone-nose bug" — the Chagas vector) is found in a Southwestern adobe home (NM, AZ, TX, southern CA). Proven on the Santa Fe adobe-house case 2026-08-17 (bug found in bathroom; mom is roommate).

## When to Use
- A resident found a kissing bug in the house / on a wall / in a bag.
- "What do I know about Chagas?" from a SW-adobe homeowner.
- Anyone asks whether a found bug is dangerous and what to do.

## Key facts (ground the reply, don't panic)
- The vector: **Western Conenose (Triatoma protracta)** — cone-shaped head, needle-like proboscis, flat body; native to NM. Lives in wall/roof cracks of adobe and rough homes; hides day, bites sleeping faces at night.
- Local presence: NM has kissing bugs and some carry *T. cruzi*; locally-acquired Chagas exists but is uncommon. One bug found ≠ infection.
- Transmission needs: bug bite + bug defecates at bite + feces rubbed into wound/mucous membranes. T. protracta defecates slowly (lower vector competence than South American species).
- Disease: acute phase often asymptomatic (2 months); ~30% of untreated infected develop chronic cardiac/GI damage years later; ~70% never serious. Benznidazole/nifurtimox treat (most effective acute/children).
- NM law: "poisoning an animal" = extreme cruelty = 4th-degree felony (NM SB0339) — relevant when residents suspect pets were poisoned.

## Response protocol (validated 2026-08-17)
1. **Preserve the specimen, don't crush.** Keep it in the zip bag; let it dry, or freeze. A testable bug beats a smear.
2. **Submit for testing** — the informative move:
   - Texas A&M Kissing Bug program: `kissingbug.tamu.edu/found-a-bug` — national submission route for NM residents (research testing). Include date, time of day, indoor/outdoor, any bites (people/pets).
   - Texas DSHS tests bugs that actually bit a person, TX residents only.
3. **Nightly bite check:** look for small welts on face/neck/arms; most bites aren't noticed. Finding a bug ≠ bitten ≠ infected.
4. **House-proof the sleep zone:**
   - Move bed off wall; tuck sheets; keep mattress from touching walls.
   - Caulk/plaster cracks in walls, baseboards, toilet base, pipe chases, roof joints (adobe = their highway system).
   - Screens, door sweeps; clear bedroom clutter; check under mattress/headboard/bedframe.
   - Keep rodents & chickens off the house perimeter (natural reservoirs attract bugs).
5. **Glue-board census** (bathroom + bedroom corners, check weekly): empty = single wanderer, done; full = learn the home has a population.
6. **Cat/dog sentinel:** if pets are getting bitten, vet consult matters (animals can be infected reservoirs).
7. **Human testing only if warranted:** real bite or repeated bugs or just want the answer — Chagas serology is a one-time blood test any PCP can order (ARUP/Quest). Not a spiral, a clean answer.

## Emotional protocol for the anxious user
- **Name the reality, then the calm:** "One bug in the house is a 'handle it' not a 'we're doomed.'" Each step in the chain (bug → bite → parasite → defecation → rub-in) drops risk dramatically.
- If the user is prone to health-anxiety loops (see `mutual-health-logging`), keep the factual ladder crisp, give action steps, offer the glue-board census as something to DO, and don't feed a research spiral — this is a real, bounded, doable problem.
- A bathroom find = transit route; a bedroom find = closer look.

## Pitfalls
- **Do not auto-convene "test everyone at zero evidence."** Blood tests are for actual exposure, not house vibes.
- **Don't answer with doom spirals** ("this is the killer house") — the honest math is on the resident's side.
- **Don't conflate kissing bugs with bedbugs or stink bugs** — ID first by cone head + prober.
- Adobe-home nuance: cracks are the feature of the architecture; sealing is ongoing maintenance, not a one-time fix.
