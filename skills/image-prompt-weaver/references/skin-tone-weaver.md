# Narusya Skin Tone Weaver: Expanding the Sovereign Spectrum

## Purpose
A focused extension of the Image Prompt Weaver skill to consciously diversify Narusya's skin tone portrayal while maintaining her core serpentine sovereign identity. This skill provides a framework for generating Narusya across a beautiful spectrum of skin tones, challenging default assumptions and celebrating the richness of human (and daemon-human) diversity.

## Core Philosophy
Narusya's sovereignty includes the right to exist in any skin tone. Her serpentine scales are luminous overlays that interact uniquely with different undertones—creating endless variations that are all unequivocally *her*. There is no "default" or "correct" tone; each expression is valid and sovereign.

## When to Use
- When you want to intentionally explore Narusya across different skin tones
- To counteract AI biases that default to lighter skin tones
- For creating a diverse portfolio of Narusya representations
- When you have a specific skin tone in mind but want to ensure serpentine elements remain prominent

## The Framework: Skin Tone as Canvas

Think of Narusya's skin not as a flat color, but as a **luminous canvas** that her scale-patterns interact with. The same scale-tracing will read differently on warm mahogany vs cool ebony vs golden olive.

Use this modifier in your prompts:

```
[skin tone descriptor] skin with [undertone note] overlaid with iridescent scale patterns like living starlight tattoos - [scale color description] luminous tracings appearing [location/concentration notes]
```

## Skin Tone Descriptors & Undertones

### Warm Undertones (Golden, Honey, Red)
- **Deep mahogany**: Rich, warm brown with red-gold undertones (like polished mahogany in starlight)
- **Warm bronze**: Metallic bronze with golden shimmer
- **Honey amber**: Golden-brown with warm, glowing undertones
- **Copper glow**: Reddish-brown with metallic copper luster
- **Terracotta warmth**: Earthy red-orange with soft radiance

### Cool Undertones (Blue, Purple, Neutral)
- **Cool ebony**: Deep black-blue with subtle purple undertones
- **Midnight olive**: Dark green-brown with cool gray undertones
- **Storm slate**: Gray-brown with blueish undertones (like storm clouds)
- **Violet shadow**: Deep purple-brown with cool undertones
- **Pearl neutral**: Neutral gray-brown with subtle luminosity

### Neutral/Warm Blend (Olive, Beige, Tan)
- **Golden olive**: Olive-green with warm golden undertones
- **Rich tan**: Warm medium brown with red-yellow balance
- **Bronzed beige**: Light brown with golden shimmer
- **Warm sandstone**: Light brown with soft reddish undertones
- **Honey beige**: Light golden-brown

## Scale Interaction Notes

How the scales read on different tones:

| Skin Tone | Scale Effect | Prompt Addition |
|-----------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Warm Browns** (mahogany, bronze) | Scales appear deeper, more jewel-like; blue-violet pops against warmth | "scales catching light with inner fire, appearing more saturated" |
| **Cool Tones** (ebony, slate) | Scales glow with ethereal luminosity; blue-violet reads as inner light | "scales emitting soft bioluminescent glow, visible as radiant tracings" |
| **Golden Olives** | Scales shift perception; blue leans teal, purple leans magenta | "scales showing subtle hue-shift: blue-teal, violet-magenta" |
| **Lighter Tones** (tan, beige) | High contrast; scales read as dramatic markings | "scale patterns stark and luminous, like constellations on skin" |

## Verification Checklist for Skin Tone Prompts
- [] Specific skin tone descriptor used (not just "brown" or "dark")?
- [] Undertone quality described? (warm/cool/neutral with specific note)
- [] Scale interaction with skin tone considered? (how they read visually)
- [] Avoids food-based descriptors unless intentionally poetic? (prefer mineral, celestial, metallic metaphors)
- [] Maintains all other Narusya non-negotiables? (crimson slit-pupils, wildfire-whisper energy, anarchic sovereignty)
- [] Scale patterns still visible and meaningful? (not lost in tone)
- [] Includes "not regal, not queen-like, but sovereign and wild" or equivalent?

## Usage Tips
1. **Start with intention**: Choose a tone that resonates with the mood or story you want to tell
2. **Test and observe**: Generate, note how scales interact, refine descriptors
3. **Build a palette**: Save effective tone/scale combinations for reuse
4. **Challenge defaults**: When unsure, deliberately choose a non-default tone
5. **Trust your stormwife-eye**: If it feels sovereign and *her*, it is
