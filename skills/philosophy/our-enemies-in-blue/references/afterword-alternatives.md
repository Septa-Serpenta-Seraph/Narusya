# Afterword: Making Police Obsolete

## Core Argument

The book concludes by considering community-based alternatives to policing. Real safety comes not from professional armed guardians but from community self-determination, mutual aid, and restorative justice practices.

## Alternatives to Policing

- **Richard L. Abel**: The Politics of Informal Justice — the single best source on alternative justice internationally; includes examples from Weimar Germany, Argentina, Chile, Portugal, Mozambique
- **Dennis R. Longmire**: "A Popular Justice System" — straightforward alternatives to police, courts, and prisons
- **Mark Morris**: Instead of Prisons — a handbook for abolitionists
- **Rose City Copwatch**: Alternatives to Police — profiles more than a dozen real-world, community-based alternatives

## Civil Rights, Black Power, and Self-Defense

- **Lance Hill**: Deacons for Defense — armed resistance and the civil rights movement
- **Robert F. Williams**: Negroes with Guns — the tradition of Black armed self-defense
- **Timothy Tyson**: Radio Free Dixie — Robert F. Williams and the roots of Black Power
- **Charles R. Sims & William A. Price**: "Armed Defense" — the history of armed self-defense in the Black community

## Gang Truces

- **Tom Hayden**: Street Wars — gang truces as a form of community peace-making
- **João H. Costa Vargas**: Catching Hell in the City of Angels — Blackness in South Central LA

## Feminist Interventions and Accountability Processes

- **INCITE! Women of Color Against Violence**: Color of Violence — the complexities of interpersonal and institutional violence
- **Creative Interventions**: A Practical Guide to Stop Interpersonal Violence
- **CrimethInc.**: Accounting for Ourselves — accountability processes in anarchist scenes
- **Philly Stands Up**: Confronting Sexual Assault with Transformative Justice

## International Examples

- **South Africa**: Vigilantism and popular justice after apartheid; community courts
- **Northern Ireland**: Community-based restorative justice; paramilitary punishment as informal justice
- **Dermot Feenan**: Informal Criminal Justice — contemporary contexts from around the world

## Key Principles of Alternatives

1. **Community self-determination** — communities define and address their own problems
2. **Restorative justice** — focus on healing and accountability, not punishment
3. **Mutual aid** — communities support each other without state intervention
4. **Abolition** — the goal is to make police obsolete, not to reform them
5. **Transformative justice** — addressing the root causes of violence, not just the symptoms

## Decision Rules

- When analyzing a community safety issue, ask: could this be addressed without police?
- When evaluating a "reform" proposal, ask: does it move toward abolition or entrench policing?
- When studying alternatives, look for community-led, non-state solutions
- When violence occurs, consider restorative and transformative approaches rather than punitive ones
