# Chapter 1: Police Brutality in Theory and Practice

## Core Argument

Police violence is not a deviation from normal operations — it IS normal operations. The institution functions through brutality, corruption, political repression, and violence. Reform rarely addresses root causes because the system is doing what it was designed to do.

## Key Frameworks

### The Prevalence of Police Violence
- Reliable information on police violence is rare; reporting is incomplete and downplays prevalence
- Best sources: Bureau of Justice Statistics, National Institute of Justice, Human Rights Watch
- Egon Bittner: "The Capacity to Use Force as the Core of the Police Role" — force is the center of policing, not an occasional tool

### Institutionalized Brutality and Police Culture
- Police culture breeds "deviant conformity" — officers learn to cover for each other
- William Westley: violence is a routine part of police work, not an aberration
- The "code of silence" is institutional, not individual

### Riots as Response to Police Violence
- Riots are not senseless — they represent the community defining for itself what counts as brutality
- Kerner Commission (1968): riots follow a pattern of cumulative grievances, almost always precipitated by police action
- James Baldwin: "The only way to police a ghetto is to be oppressive"
- Riots force issues onto the national agenda in ways petitions and op-eds cannot

## Key Cases (from the Preface/Introduction)

- **Michael Brown** (Ferguson, 2014): unarmed Black teenager shot by Officer Darren Wilson; no indictment; riots follow
- **Eric Garner** (NYC, 2014): unarmed Black man choked to death by Officer Daniel Pantaleo; "I can't breathe"; no indictment
- **Rodney King** (LA, 1991): videotaped beating by LAPD; acquittal sparks 1992 LA riots (600 fires, $1B damage, 52 dead)
- **Oscar Grant** (Oakland, 2009): unarmed Black man shot by BART officer Johannes Mehserle; riots follow

## Key Statistics/Patterns

- 105 patrol officers died on the job in 2012; policing is not among the most dangerous occupations (15.0 deaths per 100,000 vs 117.0 for truck drivers)
- 80% of police surveyed do not accept the "code of silence" — but it persists anyway
- Police kill Black people with regularity; militarization has been underway for decades

## Decision Rules

- When analyzing a police incident, ask: who benefits? Who suffers? Who is protected? Who is brutalized?
- When evaluating reform proposals: does this expand police power or reduce it?
- When a riot occurs: look for the cumulative grievances, not just the "spark"
