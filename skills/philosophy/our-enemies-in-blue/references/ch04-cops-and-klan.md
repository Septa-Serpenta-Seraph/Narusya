# Chapter 4: Cops and Klan, Hand in Hand

## Core Argument

Police and white supremacist organizations (especially the Ku Klux Klan) have a long history of collaboration and mutual support. Both serve the function of controlling Black populations and maintaining white supremacy, often with overlapping membership and shared ideology.

## The Klan and Law Enforcement

- **William Y. Chin**: "Law and Order and White Power" — white supremacist infiltration of law enforcement is documented and ongoing
- **Gladys-Marie Fry**: Night Riders in Black Folk History — slave patrols and the Klan shared tactics of supernatural intimidation, night rides, and terror
- **Centering on fear**: the Klan used the fear of the supernatural as a means of intimidation; slave patrols and Klan both operated at night

## Racist Terror as Policing

- The Klan functioned as an auxiliary police force, especially in the South
- Police and Klan shared the goal of controlling Black populations
- The Klan's violence was often ignored or actively supported by law enforcement
- **Collaboration**: police would release prisoners to the Klan, look the other way during lynchings, and participate directly in Klan activities

## Policing of Racial Minorities

- Police exist in part to stifle the social ambitions of racial minorities, especially African Americans
- **Racial profiling**: stopping, searching, and harassing Black people for minor or imagined offenses
- **Stop and frisk**: New York City's practice targeted Black and Latino men
- **Traffic stops**: racial disparities persist nationwide
- Police occupation of communities of color is reminiscent of slavery and Jim Crow

## The "War on Drugs" and Racial Disparities

- The war on drugs has been a primary mechanism for policing communities of color
- **Maryland example**: Black people represent 17% of the population but are disproportionately arrested for drug offenses
- The policing of prostitution and drug offenses falls disproportionately on women of color

## Key Statistics/Patterns

- Sexual assault and misconduct is the second most frequently reported form of police misconduct (9.3% of complaints)
- Over half of officers involved in misconduct engaged in forcible nonconsensual sexual conduct while on-duty
- Up to 2 in 5 young women report sexual harassment by law enforcement
- Women of color, low-income women, lesbian and transgender women are particularly vulnerable

## Decision Rules

- When analyzing police violence, look for the racial dimension — who is targeted?
- When evaluating a police department's relationship with the community, look for Klan/white supremantist ties
- When studying police misconduct, include sexual violence as a core category, not an edge case
- When the "war on drugs" is proposed as a solution, ask: who will be targeted?
