# Chapter 6: Police Autonomy and Blue Power

## Core Argument

Police have become a powerful political force in their own right. Through unions, associations, and the "blue wall of silence," they resist accountability and push policy toward repression. Reform efforts often expand police power rather than limit it.

## The Progressive Era and Bureaucratization

- **Edward C. Banfield & James Q. Wilson**: City Politics — the politics of reform in municipal government
- **Samuel P. Hays**: "The Politics of Reform in Municipal Government in the Progressive Era" — reform was about efficiency, not accountability
- **Max Weber**: Economy and Society — bureaucracy as a form of organization; police became bureaucracies
- **James Weinstein**: The Corporate Ideal in the Liberal State — the corporate ideal shaped police reform

## Police Unions and Blue Power

- **Algernon D. Black**: The People and the Police — chair of NYC Civilian Complaint Review Board; police resist civilian oversight
- **William J. Bopp**: The Police Rebellion — police unions as a political force; "blue power"
- **Margaret Levi**: Bureaucratic Insurgency — police unions as a form of bureaucratic insurgency
- **Robert Reiner**: The Blue-Coated Worker — police unionism and class status
- **Francis Russell**: A City in Terror — the Boston Police Strike of 1919 as a case study

## The Police as a Political Force

- Police unions create an organized political bloc that resists accountability
- The "blue wall of silence" protects officers from consequences
- Police associations lobby for harsher laws and more power
- Reform movements often co-opt police rather than constrain them

## Corporatism

- **Colin Crouch & Ronald Dore**: "Whatever Happened to Corporatism?" — police as a corporatist interest group
- **Philippe C. Schmitter**: "Still the Century of Corporatism?" — police as part of the corporatist structure

## Decision Rules

- When analyzing police reform, ask: does this expand police power or reduce it?
- When evaluating police unions, recognize them as a political force, not just a labor organization
- When studying police resistance to oversight, look for the institutional culture that enables it
- When reform is proposed, look for co-optation — does it actually change the institution?
