# Chapter 8: Riot Police or Police Riots?

## Core Argument

Riot police are not a response to disorder — they are a cause of it. The strategies used to control crowds (militarized police, preemptive arrests, "free speech zones") often escalate tensions and turn peaceful protests into violent confrontations.

## Police Strategies for Controlling Crowds

- **The Cop and the Crowd**: Police strategies for keeping the rabble in line (Clamor article)
- **Seattle WTO (1999)**: City made decisions to clear downtown streets well away from the conference facility — not to protect anyone but to gain control of the streets
- **ACLU Washington**: "Out of Control: Seattle's Flawed Response to Protests Against the World Trade Organization"
- **Seattle Police After Action Report**: The police view disorder and exaggerate its dangers

## The 1968 Democratic National Convention

- **Chicago 1968**: Police riots — the police violently attacked protestors, bystanders, and media
- **"The whole world is watching"**: The police violence was televised, creating a national crisis
- **Walker Report**: "The Chicago police department was unable to distinguish between the right of free speech and the threat of violence"

## The National Guard and Military

- **1877 Railroad Strikes**: President Hayes authorized Federal regulars; citizen's patrols organized using Civil War veterans; 5,000 special police sworn in
- **National Guard**: Created in the 1880s, partly in response to labor unrest; between 1881 and 1892 every state revised its military code to provide for an organized militia
- **Strike duty accounted for fully one-half of all deployments between 1877 and 1892**

## Free Speech Zones and Preemptive Arrests

- Police use "free speech zones" to contain and isolate protestors
- Preemptive arrests before demonstrations
- National Guard troops assigned to "training/standby" status

## The Black Panthers and Police Violence

- **Oakland police**: Fired over 2,000 rounds into a house where Eldridge Cleaver and Bobby Hutton were hiding; filled the house with tear gas; Hutton was shot and killed
- Police actively discouraged the Panthers from preventing rioting, then attacked them

## Decision Rules

- When analyzing a protest, look at police behavior — did they escalate or de-escalate?
- When "free speech zones" are established, recognize them as containment strategies
- When a "riot" occurs, examine whether police action created the violence
- When the National Guard is deployed, recognize the historical pattern of using military force against civilians
