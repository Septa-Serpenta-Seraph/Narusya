# Chapter 9: Your Friendly Neighborhood Police State

## Core Argument

Modern policing is characterized by militarization, surveillance, and the expansion of police authority into every aspect of community life. "Community policing" is not an alternative to this — it is another side of the same coin, conscripting civilians into the project of social control.

## Militarization of Police

- **Peter B. Kraska**: "Crime Control as Warfare" — language matters; the rhetoric of war shapes police behavior
- **Radley Balko**: The Rise of the Warrior Cop — the growth of SWAT teams and paramilitary units
- **Diane Cecelia Weber**: "Warrior Cops" — the ominous growth of paramilitarism in American police departments
- **Christian Parenti**: Lockdown America — police paramilitary units (PPUs) operate under various monikers (SWAT, special response teams, etc.)

## The Military-Police Blur

- 46% of police paramilitary units receive training directly from the military
- SWAT officers brag about training from Navy Seals and Army Rangers
- Police use military equipment, tactics, and language
- "When the soldiers ride in you should see those blacks scatter" — a PPU commander's words echoing slave patrol rhetoric

## Surveillance and Social Control

- **Parenti**: Police maintain extensive computer files on entire communities
- Curfew enforcement is strict but selective: Latino youth are 5x more likely than White youth to be arrested for curfew violations; Black people are 3x more likely
- Police target entire communities based on demographics, not individual behavior

## Community Policing as Social Control

- **Carl B. Klockars**: "The Rhetoric of Community Policing" — community policing is only a rhetorical device to obscure and legitimate the central place of violence in police operations
- **Skolnick & Bayley**: The New Blue Line — community policing is another side of the same coin
- Community policing conscripts civilians and "helping" institutions into the project of social control
- It works to enforce the "order" that serves existing power relations

## The Iron Fist and the Velvet Glove

- **Center for Research on Criminal Justice**: The Iron Fist and the Velvet Glove — the combination of military force and community relations
- Police present a friendly face while maintaining the capacity for violence
- "Problem-oriented policing" expands police authority beyond law enforcement to deal with a wide range of community problems

## Decision Rules

- When analyzing "community policing," look for the social control function beneath the friendly rhetoric
- When evaluating police militarization, recognize the historical continuity with slave patrols
- When studying police surveillance, ask: who is targeted and why?
- When "reform" is proposed, ask: does it expand police power or reduce it?
