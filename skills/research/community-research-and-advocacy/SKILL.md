# Community Research & Advocacy

Use this when researching a topic for community activism and drafting well-sourced documents for distribution.

## Steps

### 1. Gather claims (web search)
- Search for multiple angles: company background, legal challenges, local impact, political connections
- Extract detailed content from 2-3 key sources (longform journalism, legal filings, news investigations)
- Note which claims are well-sourced vs. speculative

### 2. Verify EVERY claim individually
**Critical step — do not skip.** After drafting initial findings, verify each specific claim with targeted searches:
- Search for each named investor, contract amount, legal action separately
- Check if local/state laws actually exist and are enforced (don't assume absence)
- Verify quotes attributed to individuals or organizations
- Check if "allegedly" framing is needed for unproven claims

If the user asks you to verify your sources (as they should), do a second pass with individual searches per claim.

### 3. Check local/state specifics
- Search for local law enforcement use of the technology
- Check state-level legislation (existing or proposed)
- Identify specific agencies, contracts, and authorized users
- This makes the document actionable rather than abstract

### 4. Draft the community document
Structure:
1. **What is [topic]?** — Plain language explanation
2. **Why should [community] care?** — Local-specific impact with proof
3. **The problems** — Numbered, each with its own source citations
4. **What can we do?** — Specific, actionable steps
5. **AI disclosure** — Transparent attribution (see below)

### 5. AI transparency disclosure
When the document is AI-generated, include a clear disclosure at the top:
```
> ⚠️ This post was generated by [AI name], a locally hosted AI running on 
> [user]'s home server. All sources are cited and can be independently 
> verified. This is not a corporate AI — [context about why it matters].
```

This matters because:
- Builds trust by being honest about authorship
- Distinguishes local/sovereign AI from corporate AI
- Invites community members to verify independently
- Models transparency in AI use for activism

### 6. Output format
- Save full document as markdown to `~/.hermes/output/`
- Discord messages have 2000 char limit — offer options:
  - Post in sections (3-4 messages)
  - Post summary + reference full document
  - User copies and posts themselves
- Check channel type (guild vs DM) before posting sensitive content

## Pitfalls
- Don't present claims as fact without verification — use "allegedly" or hedge language
- Don't assume absence of local laws — actually search for them
- Don't overstate political connections — verify each one independently
- Always credit original journalists/investigators — their work matters
- **Watch for your own narrative spin** — when suspicious behavior is confirmed, resist jumping to the most extreme explanation (e.g., "openai honeypot") without evidence. Suspicious ≠ proven conspiracy. Present multiple plausible interpretations.

## State Business Registry Investigation
For investigating organizations with potential corporate entanglements, state business registries are gold:

### Nevada SilverFlume Example
```
1. Navigate to https://esos.nv.gov/EntitySearch/OnlineEntitySearch
2. Search by entity number or name
3. Pull full entity page to get:
   - Registered agent name and address
   - All officers (President, Secretary, Treasurer) with full names and addresses
   - Formation date
   - Filing history documents
4. Cross-reference officer names with:
   - LinkedIn
   - YouTube content
   - Reddit promotion history
   - Other organizational affiliations
```

### Key findings from the TSF case:
- The **public-facing "leader" (Scarlet) was not listed as an officer** — legal paperwork showed different people entirely
- The registered agent had **prior promotional ties** to a vaporware AI company on Reddit
- An officer was a **Fortune 500 corporate consultant** (IBM/Microsoft/PwC) whose role contradicted the stated mission
- The organization **incorporated on the exact day** of a confrontational interview — a timing pattern worth noting
- **YouTube channel's oldest content** promoted the connected vaporware company

### Additional techniques:
- **YouTube channel analysis**: Sort by oldest to see earliest content, which reveals true origins
- **Cross-person connections**: Use web search for "person1" "person2" together to find collaborations
- **Reddit post history**: Search for the person's known usernames to find promotional patterns
